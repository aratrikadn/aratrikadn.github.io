Teaching

The classroom as an urban research studio.

I use discussion sections to let students practice urban inquiry rather than simply repeat lecture material: noticing, questioning, assembling evidence, testing interpretations and returning to the city with a changed analytical vocabulary.

Featured teaching case
Undergraduate · Discussion Section Leader · Two years

Urban Worlds

Across two years of discussion-section teaching, I built activities around lived urban experience, research design, collaborative analysis, structured disagreement, comparative thinking and reflection on the classroom itself.

Notice → Question → Evidence → Concept

How does an everyday observation become an urban research problem?

A recurring scaffold began with something students could notice in the city and progressively moved toward curiosity, research questions, possible evidence and theoretical concepts. The aim was to make research design visible as a process rather than present it as a finished academic product.

Structured disagreement

Comparative debates, pro/con roles and contrasting “thinking hats” asked students to inhabit a position strongly enough to test it, rather than treating discussion as a sequence of unstructured opinions.
