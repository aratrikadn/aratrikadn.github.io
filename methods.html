Methods

How I think and work.

Methods are not a toolbox detached from the research question. I use them to retain complexity: narrative and affect, collective interpretation, overlapping quantitative explanations, and the spatial contexts in which social processes unfold.

01

Qualitative

Telesketching · Narrative · Visual Ethnography

I use qualitative inquiry to understand how people narrate uncertainty, place, care, adaptation and mobility. In my Sundarbans work, telesketching translates spoken testimony into annotated sketches, attending to affect, pauses, metaphor, sensory detail and the social and spatial relations embedded in memory.

02

Participatory

Learning · Mapping · Co-production

I use participatory methods in both teaching and fieldwork, treating knowledge as something that can be assembled, debated and revised collectively rather than simply extracted or delivered.

Teaching

Affinity mapping, structured debates, collaborative feedback, visual synthesis and collateral learning turn the classroom into a site of collective inquiry.

Fieldwork

Participatory urban inquiry works with residents as producers of spatial knowledge through observation, dialogue, collaborative interpretation and mapping.

03

Quantitative

Patterns · Interactions · Heterogeneity

I use quantitative methods to examine patterns that are difficult to see through individual cases alone. My work combines data construction, regression and interaction models, multidimensional indices, clustering, comparative analysis and diagnostic visualization, with particular attention to heterogeneity and overlapping explanations.

04

GIS & Spatial Analysis

Movement · Exposure · Environmental Change

I use GIS to connect social processes with their spatial and environmental contexts. My work combines tabular and spatial data, migration-flow visualization, layered displacement and settlement data, and satellite-based raster analysis, including Sentinel-2 imagery and NDWI calculations for flood exposure.
